Overview & Key Concepts

Dependent censoring arises when the survival time \(T\) and the censoring time \(C\) are not independent. Because we only observe \(Y = \min(T,C)\) and \(\Delta = I(T \le C)\), the joint distribution of \((T,C)\) is not identifiable without additional structural assumptions. Applying standard methods (KM, Cox) under dependent censoring produces biased estimates.

Core Observable We see either \(T\) or \(C\) — never both — so the dependence between them cannot be recovered from data alone. A structural model (parametric or semiparametric) is required to identify the event-time distribution.

Introduction to Dependent Censoring

When Is \(T \perp\!\!\!\perp C\) Plausible?

  • Administrative censoring: individuals alive at the pre-specified end date are censored — determined by the study calendar, unrelated to individual health.
  • Unrelated loss to follow-up: patients who move away or die in unrelated accidents.

When Is \(T \perp\!\!\!\perp C\) Doubtful?

  • Medical trials (deteriorating): patients withdraw because their condition is worsening → positive association (\(T\) short, \(C\) short).
  • Medical trials (improved): patients withdraw because they feel better and stop treatment → negative association.
  • Unemployment studies: unmotivated individuals drop out quickly and also take longer to find work → \(C\) depends on \(T\).
  • Transplant studies: organ allocation is based on disease severity, so waiting time \(C\) depends on survival prognosis \(T\).
  • Health economics (medical costs): if \(U = RT\) and each individual has a random cost-accumulation rate \(R\), then \(U\) is censored by \(RC\), creating dependence through the shared \(R\).
Critical Principle \(T \perp\!\!\!\perp C\) cannot be tested nonparametrically from \((Y_i, \Delta_i)\) alone. It must be motivated by the study context. Standard methods applied under dependent censoring produce biased and potentially misleading results.

Copulas

Definition and Sklar's Theorem

A copula is a bivariate distribution function on \([0,1]^2\) with uniform margins. It captures the dependence structure separately from the marginals.

Sklar's Theorem
If \(X \sim F\), \(Y \sim G\) are continuous, there exists a unique copula \(\mathcal{C}\) such that \[ P(X \le x,\; Y \le y) = \mathcal{C}(F(x),\, G(y)). \] If \(X \perp\!\!\!\perp Y\), then \(\mathcal{C}(u,v) = uv\) — the independence copula.

Archimedean Copulas

A copula is Archimedean if \(\mathcal{C}(u,v) = \psi^{[-1]}(\psi(u)+\psi(v))\), where \(\psi:[0,1]\to[0,\infty]\) is the generator — continuous, strictly decreasing, convex, \(\psi(1)=0\) — and \(\psi^{[-1]}\) is its pseudo-inverse.

Gaussian (Elliptical) Copula

\[ \mathcal{C}_\rho(u,v) = \Phi_R\!\left(\Phi^{-1}(u),\;\Phi^{-1}(v)\right), \] where \(\Phi_R\) is the bivariate normal CDF with correlation matrix \(R\) and \(\Phi^{-1}\) is the standard normal quantile function. The parameter \(\rho \in [-1,1]\) controls the strength and direction of dependence.

Kendall's Tau

The association parameter \(\xi\) of a copula family can be linked to Kendall's tau: \[ \tau = 4\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^1 \mathcal{C}_\xi(u,v)\,d\mathcal{C}_\xi(u,v) - 1. \] This relationship is one-to-one for Archimedean and Gaussian copulas, allowing a simple summary of dependence strength.

H-Functions (Conditional CDFs)

The h-functions are partial derivatives of the copula and represent conditional CDFs: \[ h_{T|C}(u\mid v) = \frac{\partial}{\partial v}\mathcal{C}(u,v), \qquad h_{C|T}(v\mid u) = \frac{\partial}{\partial u}\mathcal{C}(u,v). \] These appear directly in the likelihood under dependent censoring.

Models Without Covariates

Non-identifiability

Cox (1959) and Tsiatis (1975): for any observed \((Y,\Delta)\) process, infinitely many joint distributions \((T,C)\) are compatible with it. Crowder (1991): even if the marginal laws are known, the joint distribution is not identifiable. This necessitates structural assumptions.

Approach 1 — Zheng & Klein (1995): Known Copula

Model the joint survival via a fully specified copula (known family and parameter value): \[ P(T>t,\;C>c) = \mathcal{C}(S_T(t),\,S_C(c)). \] Extends the KM estimator to dependent censoring. The copula must be fully pre-specified.

Approach 2 — Rivest & Wells (2001): Copula-Graphic Estimator

For Archimedean copulas with \(\psi(0)=\infty\): \[ P(T>t,\;C>c) = \psi^{-1}\!\left(\psi(S_T(t))+\psi(S_C(c))\right). \] The copula-graphic estimator has an explicit formula: \[ \hat{S}_T(t) = \psi^{-1}\!\left[-\sum_{Y_i\le t,\,\Delta_i=1}\!\left\{\psi\!\left(\hat\pi(Y_i)\right)-\psi\!\left(\hat\pi(Y_i)-\tfrac{1}{n}\right)\right\}\right], \] where \(\hat\pi(t) = n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n I(Y_i \ge t)\).

Special Case: KM as Copula-Graphic Setting \(\psi(u)=-\log u\) (independence copula) makes the copula-graphic estimator reduce exactly to the Kaplan–Meier estimator. KM is a special case under independence. 
library(compound.Cox); library(copula)
tau = tau(claytonCopula(2))       # tau = alpha/(alpha+2) = 2/4 = 0.50
fit.cg = CG.Clayton(Y, Delta, alpha = 2)   # copula-graphic estimator

# Compare different tau values
alpha = c(0, 1, 2, 4)            # tau = 0, 1/3, 1/2, 2/3
for (k in 1:4) {
  fit.cg = CG.Clayton(Y, Delta, alpha = alpha[k], S.plot = FALSE)
}
Sensitivity Warning The copula-graphic estimator is highly sensitive to the choice of \(\tau\). Different values of \(\tau\) yield substantially different estimated survival functions. This motivates methods that estimate \(\tau\) from the data rather than fix it in advance.

Approach 3 — Czado & Van Keilegom (2023): Unknown Copula Parameter

Both marginals and copula are parametric, but the copula parameter \(\theta\) is unknown: \[ F_{T,C}(t,c) = \mathcal{C}_\theta(F_{T,\theta_T}(t),\;F_{C,\theta_C}(c)). \] The full parameter vector \(\alpha = (\theta, \theta_T, \theta_C)^\top\) is estimated by MLE.

Identifiability Theorem (Czado & VK, 2023)
The model is identifiable if conditions (C1) and (C2) hold:
  • (C1) Marginal tail condition: \(\lim_{t\to a} f_{T,\theta_{T1}}(t)/f_{T,\theta_{T2}}(t) = 1 \iff \theta_{T1}=\theta_{T2}\) for \(a\in\{0,\infty\}\), and similarly for \(C\). Satisfied for log-normal, log-Student-\(t\), Weibull, log-logistic.
  • (C2) H-function tail condition: \(\lim_{t\to 0\text{ or }\infty} h_{T|C,\theta}(u_t\mid v_t)=0\) for all parameters. Satisfied for Frank (always), Gumbel (mild tail conditions on margins), Gaussian (mild conditions).

Log-Likelihood

For i.i.d. data \(\{(Y_i,\Delta_i)\}\): \begin{align} \ell(\alpha;\mathcal{D}) &= \sum_{\Delta_i=1}\log\!\left(f_{T,\theta_T}(Y_i)\bigl\{1-h_{C|T,\theta}(F_{C,\theta_C}(Y_i)\mid F_{T,\theta_T}(Y_i))\bigr\}\right) \\ &\quad + \sum_{\Delta_i=0}\log\!\left(f_{C,\theta_C}(Y_i)\bigl\{1-h_{T|C,\theta}(F_{T,\theta_T}(Y_i)\mid F_{C,\theta_C}(Y_i))\bigr\}\right). \end{align}

Simulation Summary (Frank copula, log-normal margins) For \(n=200\) and \(n=500\), the MLE shows near-zero bias for all parameters including Kendall's \(\tau\). Larger \(\tau\) is estimated more precisely (smaller SD/RMSE). Asymptotic SEs from the Hessian closely match empirical SDs. Estimators are approximately normal.

Approach 2 — Transformation Model (Deresa & VK, 2020)

Both \(T\) and \(C\) are modelled jointly through a parametric transformation: \[ \begin{cases} \Lambda_\theta(\log T) = \beta_0 + \beta^\top X + \epsilon_T \\ \Lambda_\theta(\log C) = \eta_0 + \eta^\top W + \epsilon_C \end{cases} \] with \(\binom{\epsilon_T}{\epsilon_C} \sim N_2\!\!\left(\mathbf{0},\;\Sigma = \begin{pmatrix}\sigma_T^2 & \rho\sigma_T\sigma_C \\ \rho\sigma_T\sigma_C & \sigma_C^2\end{pmatrix}\right)\) independent of \((X,W)\). The parameter \(\rho = \text{Corr}(\Lambda_\theta(\log T), \Lambda_\theta(\log C)\mid X,W) \in [-1,1]\) directly quantifies the residual dependence.

Yeo–Johnson Transformation

\[ \Lambda_\theta(t) = \begin{cases} \{(t+1)^\theta-1\}/\theta & t \ge 0,\;\theta\ne 0 \\ \log(t+1) & t \ge 0,\;\theta=0 \\ -\{(-t+1)^{2-\theta}-1\}/(2-\theta) & t<0,\;\theta\ne 2 \\ -\log(-t+1) & t<0,\;\theta=2 \end{cases} \]

  • \(\theta = 1\): \(\Lambda_1(\log T) = \log T\) — log-normal marginal.
  • \(1 < \theta \le 2\): convex, lies above \(\log T\).
  • \(0 \le \theta < 1\): concave, lies below \(\log T\).

Generalises Box–Cox to the full real line: Yeo-Johnson maps \(\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\) for \(\theta \in [0,2]\), while Box-Cox maps only \(\mathbb{R}^+\).

Approach 3 — Semiparametric Copula Model (Deresa & VK, 2024)

\(T|X\) follows a Cox PH model: \[ F_{T|X}(t|x) = 1-\exp\!\{-H_0(t)e^{x^\top\beta}\}, \] \(C|W\) follows a parametric regression model, and an independent administrative censoring time \(A\) is also possible, so we observe \(Y=\min(T,C,A)\), \(\Delta_1=I(Y=T)\), \(\Delta_2=I(Y=C)\). The copula \(\mathcal{C}_\gamma\) links \(T\) and \(C\) (independently of covariates).

Identifiability (C1)–(C5)
Under conditions including: full-rank covariance of \(X\) and \(W\); at least one unique continuous variable in each; tail conditions on marginals and copula — the model is identifiable for Frank, Gumbel, and Gaussian copulas paired with log-normal, Weibull, etc., marginals.

Estimation — Pseudo-Likelihood Approach

Direct maximisation is difficult because \(H_0\) is unknown. Instead:

  1. For fixed \(\theta = (\gamma,\beta,\eta)\), compute the Breslow estimator \(\hat{H}_0(\cdot,\theta)\).
  2. Plug into the score equation: \(U_n(\theta,\hat{H}_0(\cdot,\theta)) = 0\).
  3. Solve for \(\hat\theta\).
Connection to Cox Partial Likelihood Under the independence copula \(\mathcal{C}(u,v)=uv\), \(\hat{H}_0\) reduces to the Breslow estimator and \(\hat\theta\) reduces exactly to the Cox partial likelihood estimator. The new method is a genuine extension of Cox to dependent censoring.

Simulations (Frank copula, \(n=500\), \(\tau=0.8\)): the Frank copula model has near-zero bias (e.g. \(\hat\beta_2\): bias = −0.013); the naive independence copula (Cox) has Bias = 0.327 for \(\beta_2\) — a 32% overestimation of the covariate effect. Estimators are approximately normal (confirmed by QQ plots).

Question 26 · Archimedean Copula — Generator Property
A valid Archimedean generator \(\psi: [0,1] \to [0,\infty]\) must satisfy which properties?
The generator must be: continuous, strictly decreasing (copula must decrease in each argument away from independence), convex, and \(\psi(1) = 0\) (so that \(\mathcal{C}(u,1) = \psi^{[-1]}(\psi(u)+0) = u\), ensuring uniform margins). These are the standard conditions from the slides.
Question 27 · Transplant Study — Direction of Rho
In the liver transplant study: "Patients who get a transplant tend to be those who are closer to death." Based on this clinical fact alone (before fitting any model), we would expect \(\rho\) to be:
Correct clinical reasoning: sicker → shorter \(T\) and shorter \(C\) simultaneously → positive correlation. The fitted model confirms this with \(\hat\rho = 0.741\). This pre-fitting prediction from scientific reasoning is exactly how one "motivates" the dependence structure before analysis.
Question 28 · Yeo–Johnson vs. Box–Cox
A key advantage of the Yeo–Johnson transformation over Box–Cox in the context of the Deresa & VK (2020) model is:
Box–Cox maps \(\mathbb{R}^+ \to (-1/\theta, \infty)\), requiring strictly positive inputs. Since \(\log T \in \mathbb{R}\), Box–Cox cannot be directly applied. Yeo–Johnson extends Box–Cox to \(\mathbb{R}\), making it suitable for transforming \(\log T\). This is explicitly stated in the slides.
Question 29 · Extensions — True/False
True or False: The transformation model of Deresa & VK (2020) can be extended to competing risks by using different Yeo–Johnson transformation parameters for each competing cause of failure.
True. The slides explicitly list this as an extension: "Extension to competing risks (with different transformations for each risk), and to regimes with independent (administrative) and dependent censoring." Using cause-specific transformations adds flexibility to model different hazard shapes per cause.
